1. THE STANDARD OF PERFECTION: THEORETICAL FOUNDATIONS

CHTPML (PieceMark Markup Language):
The "Soul" of TPMOS. Perfection is defined by absolute auditability and file-backed sovereignty. If a variable exists on screen, its source must be discoverable in a state file. Navigation is deterministic (numeric jumps).

CHTMGL (PieceMark Graphics/Grid Language):
The "Brain" of TPMOS. Perfection is the semantic bridge. It is not just about drawing; it is about describing intent (x, y, z, role, color). A perfect CHTMGL parser treats ASCII and RGB as twin projections of the same underlying scene tree.

GLOS (OpenGL Operating System):
The "Theater" of TPMOS. Perfection is sovereign orchestration. It manages the lifecycle of windows and processes, routing input at high frequency (60fps) to prevent lag, while maintaining the "One Writer" rule for project state.

2. THE WRAITH INTENTION

Wraith is the synthesis of these three. It seeks to bring GLOS-grade window management and process orchestration into the auditable ASCII frame standard.
- Goal: A desktop shell that is visible in CLI, convertible to RGB, and navigable by both keyboard (numeric/arrow) and mouse.
- Perfection: The "Mousehand" cursor acts as the unifying indicator, snapping to navigation focus while tracking mouse coordinates, ensuring the user always knows their point of interaction regardless of input mode.

4. THE "MOUSEHAND" & NAVIGATION STRATEGY

The 'mousehand' is more than a cursor; it is a focus-lock indicator.
- Mouse Mode: '+' follows `mouse_x/y` exactly. Hit testing against `hitmap.pdl` updates `focused_window` and `focused_control`.
- Nav Mode: Arrow keys and numeric jumps move the selection. The 'mousehand' SNAPS to the center of the newly focused object/cell, providing visual confirmation of the jump.
- Implementation: Reference `3.tui_explorer]MOUSE.c` for ANSI mouse reporting (`\x1b[?1000h`) and coordinate extraction.
